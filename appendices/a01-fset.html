Appendix A — A1. The Financial Statement Effects Template

The FSET traces one event across the accounting equation, income statement, and cash-flow statement. This appendix completes the operating-company example in Section 5.5. It does not use the earlier present-value model’s balances.

A.1 How to read a row

Use signed changes. Revenue increases income and equity; expense decreases them. The income columns explain the change in equity; do not add income to equity again when checking the equation. An increase in accumulated depreciation reduces net assets.

Always check:

\[\Delta A=\Delta L+\Delta E.\]

A balanced row is necessary for consistency. It does not prove that the transaction occurred or that its measurement is correct.

A.2 December transactions before adjustment

The company starts with zero balances. Cash-flow categories are operating (O), investing (I), and financing (F).

Event Assets Liabilities Equity Revenue Expense Cash flow
Owners contribute Cash +8,000 — +8,000 — — +8,000 F
Borrow Cash +10,000 Note +10,000 — — — +10,000 F
Buy equipment Equipment +3,600; cash −3,600 — — — — −3,600 I
Prepay insurance Prepaid +3,000; cash −3,000 — — — — −3,000 O
Buy supplies Supplies +1,200; cash −1,200 — — — −1,200 O
Collect subscription advance Cash +5,000 Unearned revenue +5,000 — — — +5,000 O

After these transactions, assets are 23,000 (cash 15,200, equipment 3,600, prepaid insurance 3,000, supplies 1,200); liabilities are 15,000; equity is 8,000. Income is zero at this intermediate, unadjusted stage.

A.3 Seven adjustments

Adjustment Net assets Liabilities Equity via income Revenue Expense Cash
Insurance used −1,000 — −1,000 — +1,000 0
Depreciation −100 — −100 — +100 0
Subscription service provided — −1,250 +1,250 +1,250 — 0
Supplies used −900 — −900 — +900 0
Delivery on account +800 — +800 +800 — 0
Wages owed — +1,500 −1,500 — +1,500 0
Interest owed — +50 −50 — +50 0
Total adjustment −1,200 +300 −1,500 +2,050 +3,550 0

The total row balances: −1,200 = +300 − 1,500. Adjusted assets are 21,800, liabilities 15,300, and equity 6,500. Cash remains 15,200. The net loss is 1,500.

A.4 Translate the same effects into journal entries

A debit is the left side of an entry; a credit is the right. They do not mean good and bad, or necessarily increase and decrease. Appendix A3 gives the account rules.

Event Debit Credit
Owners contribute Cash 8,000 Contributed capital 8,000
Borrow Cash 10,000 Notes payable 10,000
Buy equipment Equipment 3,600 Cash 3,600
Prepay insurance Prepaid insurance 3,000 Cash 3,000
Buy supplies Supplies 1,200 Cash 1,200
Subscription advance Cash 5,000 Unearned revenue 5,000
Insurance adjustment Insurance expense 1,000 Prepaid insurance 1,000
Depreciation adjustment Depreciation expense 100 Accumulated depreciation 100
Subscription adjustment Unearned revenue 1,250 Service revenue 1,250
Supplies adjustment Supplies expense 900 Supplies 900
Completed delivery Accounts receivable 800 Service revenue 800
Accrued wages Wages expense 1,500 Wages payable 1,500
Accrued interest Interest expense 50 Interest payable 50

Contributed capital combines the owners’ contributed-equity accounts here; splitting common stock and additional paid-in capital is not required in this opening case.

A.6 Optional: closing and the operating cash reconciliation

To close directly to retained earnings, debit service revenue 2,050 and credit retained earnings 2,050; then debit retained earnings 3,550 and credit each expense account for its balance. Temporary revenue and expense accounts become zero. Retained earnings has a debit balance of 1,500. These entries do not move cash or create an additional loss.

The indirect operating-cash reconciliation is:

Item Amount
Net loss −1,500
Add noncash depreciation +100
Increase in receivables −800
Increase in supplies −300
Increase in prepaid insurance −2,000
Increase in unearned revenue +3,750
Increase in wages payable +1,500
Increase in interest payable +50
Operating cash flow 800

The result equals cash collected from customers (5,000) less insurance (3,000) and supplies (1,200) payments. The main Week 5 requirement uses the direct cash-flow calculation. Closing procedures and the indirect reconciliation are extensions revisited later.

# A1. The Financial Statement Effects Template {#sec-fset .appendix}

The FSET traces one event across the accounting equation, income statement, and cash-flow statement. This appendix completes the operating-company example in @sec-first-accruals. It does not use the earlier present-value model's balances.

## How to read a row

Use signed changes. Revenue increases income and equity; expense decreases them. The income columns **explain** the change in equity; do not add income to equity again when checking the equation. An increase in accumulated depreciation reduces net assets.

Always check:

$$\Delta A=\Delta L+\Delta E.$$

A balanced row is necessary for consistency. It does not prove that the transaction occurred or that its measurement is correct.

## December transactions before adjustment

The company starts with zero balances. Cash-flow categories are operating (O), investing (I), and financing (F).

| Event | Assets | Liabilities | Equity | Revenue | Expense | Cash flow |
|---|---|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Owners contribute | Cash +8,000 | — | +8,000 | — | — | +8,000 F |
| Borrow | Cash +10,000 | Note +10,000 | — | — | — | +10,000 F |

| Collect subscription advance | Cash +5,000 | Unearned revenue +5,000 | — | — | — | +5,000 O |

After these transactions, assets are 23,000 (cash 15,200, equipment 3,600, prepaid insurance 3,000, supplies 1,200); liabilities are 15,000; equity is 8,000. Income is zero at this intermediate, unadjusted stage.
