12.1 A report rewards some activities more than others

A manager wants staff to solve customers’ problems. A count of completed calls is cheap to collect. Paying for completed calls may encourage faster service; it may also reward ending difficult calls too soon. The count and the organization’s objective differ.

Holmstrom and Milgrom (1991) studies incentives when agents perform several tasks and performance is measured unevenly. When tasks compete for effort, stronger rewards for one measured task can divert effort from another valuable task. Lower-powered rewards can then be useful. The conclusion depends on task substitution, costs, measurement and the other incentives workers face. It is not a universal instruction to eliminate bonuses or reject better monitoring.

12.2 A constructed two-task choice

An employee chooses exactly one of two equally costly work plans. A base wage is the same under either. Treat outcomes as known for this example.

Work plan Completed sales Quality checks Organization value: 10 per sale + 20 per check
Balanced 6 4 140
Sales-focused 10 0 100

At $5 per sale and nothing for checks, variable pay is 30 versus 50, so the employee chooses sales-focused work. At $3 per sale plus $5 per verified check, variable pay is 38 versus 30, favoring balanced work. That redesign requires checks to be verifiable at an acceptable cost. If checks can be fabricated or measured unreliably, the problem changes.

Reducing the sale-only bonus from $5 to $1 leaves pay at 6 versus 10, so it does not change the choice in this particular two-plan example. At a flat wage the employee is indifferent under the specified payoffs; balanced effort does not follow without another motivation or rule. A useful theory identifies conditions under which a reform works.

12.3 Wells Fargo: apply the mechanism to evidence

In September 2016 the CFPB reported unauthorized account practices at Wells Fargo and linked them to sales goals and compensation incentives. The action required refunds and a $100 million CFPB penalty; contemporaneous combined fines from the CFPB, OCC and Los Angeles totaled $185 million. These are historical enforcement findings, not a controlled comparison of incentive systems. CFPB action summary and September 8, 2016 remarks.

Identify the rewarded measure, the customer outcome it could miss, behavior that changes the measure, and controls that could detect it. Reducing pressure on account counts, checking customer authorization independently and protecting escalation channels address different parts of the problem. Evaluate the benefit and cost of each. Do not assert that a particular reform would certainly have prevented every violation.

In class, write your diagnosis and two proposed reforms before AI. Then critique an AI answer or a supplied printed answer. Credit a sound answer when it is sound. Better measurement is not automatically inconsistent with multitasking theory.

12.4 ROI and residual income use a specified accounting basis

For required cases, taxes are ignored, operating profit and average operating assets are supplied, and the required return is on that same stated basis:

\[ROI=\frac{Operating\ profit}{Average\ operating\ assets};\qquad RI=Operating\ profit-r(Average\ operating\ assets).\]

A division earns $40 million on average operating assets of $200 million. The supplied capital charge is 10%. ROI is 20% and RI is 20 million. A project adds profit $7 million and average operating assets $50 million. Its accounting return is 14%. After acceptance, division ROI becomes 47/250 = 18.8%; RI becomes 47−.10(250) = 22 million.

If paid solely to raise this year’s ROI, the manager sees a lower rewarded metric. If paid solely to raise RI, the project adds 2 million. These are predictions under the stated contract; career concerns, future rewards and nonfinancial goals can change behavior.

The calculation does not alone establish NPV. To connect the two, supply a cash-flow model. Suppose the project pays 7 million cash at year-end, returns its entire 50 million investment then, and has no other cash flows. Average operating assets are defined as 50 million for the full operating period; no depreciation is needed because capital is fully recovered. NPV = −50 + 57/1.10 = 1.818 million. In this deliberately specified one-period case, positive RI and NPV recommendations agree. For a depreciating multi-year asset, use the full timeline; one year’s accounting return is insufficient.

RI is in dollars and ROI is a rate. A large division can earn more RI with a lower ROI than a small division. Compare opportunities and changes within context rather than rank manager ability from either number alone. Asset age, measurement policies and controllability affect both.

12.5 A materials-variance bridge, step by step

A standard says each completed unit should use 3 pounds at $5 per pound. Actual output is 2,000 units. The firm purchases and uses 6,300 pounds at $5.30; no materials inventory changes. The standard is a benchmark, not an observed fact about what production must require.

Standard quantity allowed for actual output = 2,000 × 3 = 6,000 pounds. Allowed cost = 6,000 × $5 = 30,000. Actual cost = 6,300 × $5.30 = 33,390. The 3,390 unfavorable difference decomposes as:

Step Formula Result
Price variance (Actual price − standard price) × actual quantity (.30)(6,300) = 1,890 U
Usage variance (Actual quantity − allowed quantity) × standard price (300)(5) = 1,500 U
Total Price + usage 3,390 U

For costs, “unfavorable” means higher than this benchmark, not automatically a bad decision. Better material might cost more but reduce failures. Cheaper material might cause more waste. Investigate price and usage together.

Price variance is sometimes recorded on quantity purchased, while usage variance uses quantity used. Week 12 Drill 1 gives purchases and Drill 2 independently gives usage. Do not add these separate drills and call the total a materials-used reconciliation. Our worked bridge sets purchases equal to usage so it does reconcile.

12.6 Design a report around a decision

Begin with the desired activity and who can change it. Choose measures, explain their scope and propose a check on an omitted dimension. Compare benefits with collection costs. A balanced scorecard adds financial and nonfinancial measures, but weighting, measurement and gaming remain design choices. A favorable variance does not identify a good manager without a credible standard and context.

Self-check. Profit 600,000, average assets 3,000,000 and required return 14% give ROI 20% and RI 180,000. If a quality audit is added, identify a behavior it verifies and another it still misses. There is no unique correct portfolio without specifying the setting.

12.7 Drill Set

Required: 1–2 and 5–6. Optional: 3–4 and 7. Complete the required items after instruction by April 11, 2027, 11:59 p.m. Eastern. Work individually by hand, check the key, and correct errors. Graded for completion; optional items are excluded from quizzes and the final.

Label variances favorable (F) or unfavorable (U). Items 1 and 2 use different quantities; do not add them as one total usage-cost variance.

  1. (Materials price variance.) The standard price is $5.00 per pound; the actual price is $5.30 per pound; the actual quantity purchased is 8,000 pounds. Compute the direct-materials price variance.

  2. (Materials quantity variance.) The standard is 3 pounds per unit at $5.00 per pound; 2,000 units are produced; actual usage is 6,300 pounds. Compute the direct-materials quantity variance.

  3. (Labor rate variance.) The standard rate is $20.00 per hour; the actual rate is $19.50 per hour; actual hours worked total 4,200. Compute the direct-labor rate variance.

  4. (Labor efficiency variance.) The standard is 2 hours per unit at $20.00 per hour; 2,000 units are produced; actual hours total 4,200. Compute the direct-labor efficiency variance.

  5. (Return on investment.) A division reports operating income of $600,000 and average operating assets of $3,000,000. Compute ROI.

  6. (Residual income.) For the same division (operating income $600,000; operating assets $3,000,000), the required rate of return is 14%. Compute residual income.

  7. (Economic value added.) A firm has NOPAT of $1,200,000, invested capital of $8,000,000, and a weighted-average cost of capital of 10%. Compute EVA.

Drill Set — Self-Check Answers

Required answers: 1–2 and 5–6. Other answers support optional practice.

  1. Materials price variance. = (actual price − standard price) × actual quantity = (5.30 − 5.00) × 8,000 = $2,400 U.

  2. Materials quantity variance. Standard quantity allowed = 2,000 × 3 = 6,000 lbs. Variance = (actual − standard) × standard price = (6,300 − 6,000) × 5.00 = $1,500 U.

  3. Labor rate variance. = (actual rate − standard rate) × actual hours = (19.50 − 20.00) × 4,200 = $2,100 F.

  4. Labor efficiency variance. Standard hours allowed = 2,000 × 2 = 4,000. Variance = (actual − standard) × standard rate = (4,200 − 4,000) × 20.00 = $4,000 U.

  5. ROI. = 600,000 ÷ 3,000,000 = 20%.

  6. Residual income. = operating income − (required rate × operating assets) = 600,000 − 0.14 × 3,000,000 = 600,000 − 420,000 = $180,000.

  7. EVA. = NOPAT − (WACC × invested capital) = 1,200,000 − 0.10 × 8,000,000 = 1,200,000 − 800,000 = $400,000.

12.8 Optional extension: EVA and formal models

An EVA-style measure subtracts a capital charge from adjusted net operating profit after tax (NOPAT), using invested capital and a compatible weighted-average cost of capital. RI does not intrinsically require cost of equity: the required return must match the profit and capital basis. Do not improve a comparison merely by choosing a lower rate.

For a supplied adjustment, begin with NOPAT 100 and invested capital 500, in millions; WACC is 10%. Unadjusted residual operating profit is 50. Suppose after-tax current R&D expense is 20, amortization would be 12, and the supplied average unamortized R&D capital is 60. Adjusted NOPAT is 100+20−12 = 108; adjusted capital is 560; the measure is 108−.10(560) = 52. This is sensitivity to an accounting model, not a discovered market value. Do not add operating leases again if already included in the capital base.

Labor variances apply analogous rate/efficiency decompositions. The formal Holmstrom–Milgrom model and Chapter 4’s optional LEN derivation deepen the incentive conditions. These extensions are excluded from Quiz 4 and the final.

Holmstrom, Bengt, and Paul Milgrom. 1991. “Multitask Principal-Agent Analyses: Incentive Contracts, Asset Ownership, and Job Design.” Journal of Law, Economics, and Organization 7: 24–52.
